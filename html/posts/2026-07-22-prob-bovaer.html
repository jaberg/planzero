Introduction

PlanZero's Modelling a Bovaer Strategy post from earlier this year estimated that a strategy of administering the feed additive Bovaer could remove up to 10 Mt{{CO2e}} annually at a cost of $222.94/t{{CO2e}}. Intuitively, the trouble with that estimate is that it is too precise to be credible. Formally, the trouble with that estimate is that it isn't really a {{siteref("Prediction", "prediction")|safe}} at all, it's just a number. Several subsequent posts have been developing a probabilistic modelling framework for PlanZero. The purpose of the probabilistic approach is to be able to state, with the support of appropriate data and assumptions, proper predictions (probability distributions) for such statistics / metrics as the {{siteref("Emission_Reduction_Efficiency", "emissions reduction efficiency")|safe}} of a strategy. A proper prediction regarding the impact of Bovaer would tell us whether that figure of $222.94 is accurate; it would tell us with what probability that figure is accurate to the nearest cent, the nearest dollar, the nearest one hundred dollars, and so on.

This post is about how PlanZero's probabilistic models can estimate the impact of a strategy and conclude instead that based on (a) the assumptions of the Static Normals model, and (b) additional structural assumptions related to a hypothetical Bovaer program, the cost per tonne {{CO2}}e abated over the modelled period lie in the range 188.99 - 200.08 (CAD / t{{CO2}}e) with 95% confidence. This is lower than the deterministic estimate of $222/t {{CO2}}e in the previous post because of the recognition of income tax revenue.

A Scaling Model

The {{siteref("Static_Normals_Model", "Static Normals model")|safe}} (introduced in this post) models the emission of each gas from each NIR sector indepenently. It is therefore possible to extend it to model a strategy for emissions reduction by replacing one of the emissions sectors, and leaving the rest as static normals. This post is about replacing the Static Normals modelling of the Enteric Fermentation sector. Whereas the Static Normals model estimates emissions in the sector with regional [static] normal distributions, the Scaling model, introduced with this post, models Enteric Fermentation emissions instead with unknown variables relating to how Bovaer might influence emissions. The new variables include the following:

  • cattle head-counts, per province and territory, per cattle type
  • per-head methane emission factors, per cattle type
  • fraction of farms administering Bovaer

The assumptions how about these variables are defined, and how they relate to Enteric Fermentation emissions are encoded in new {{siteref("Model_Element", "model elements")|safe}}. The new elements are outlined in Appendix 1, and presented mathematically in Appendix 2. As before, the "Scale Bovaer" strategy implements the idea that some fraction of cattle farmers would be open to administering Bovaer if they were sufficiently incentivized by government subsidy. The model assumes, as before, that a sufficient incentive would be $5000 per year. Differences relative to the previous deterministic model:

  • Whereas the deterministic model fixed the fraction of farmers-open-to-switching-per-year at 5%, the probabilistic model varies it from 2.5-10% uniformly.
  • Whereas the deterministic model fixed Bovaer production emissions at 45 kg {{CO2}} / head / year, the probabilistic model varies it uniformly, from 20x to 50x less than the abated methane emissions (in terms of {{CO2e}}).
  • Whereas the deterministic model ignored income tax, the probabilistic model includes the government recouping it at a rate of 25% from amounts paid as salaries or subsidies.

Conclusion

This post has introduced a probabilistic Scaling model, which is a proof of concept that (1) it is possible to extend a probabilistic model (the Static Normals model) with a strategy, and (2) estimate the impact of the strategy by ablative analysis. The result is a proper probabilistic prediction of the impact of a strategy, that estimates the probabilities of all possible values of unknown variables, especially e.g. the amounts of methane abated, and the emissions reduction efficiency. The strategy page for "Scale Bovaer" currently features the figures above.

In future work, I intend to improve the Scaling model iteratively. First, I would like to upgrade it to use an autoregressive baseline for each sector instead of static normals [edit: this post]. Beyond that, I would like to incorporate more bespoke sector models that include additional data sources and strategies, similarly to how this post incorporated details related to Enteric Fermentation. However, before making these iterative improvements I would like to finalize the glossary and perform significant clean-up and refactoring of PlanZero's source code so that those iterative changes can be easier and more systematic [edit: this post].

Until then,

- James Bergstra

Appendix 1: Model Elements to Model Bovaer Adoption

The Scaling Model is identical to the {{siteref("Static_Normals_Model", "Static Normals")|safe}} model in every sector except Enteric Fermentation and Other Product Manufacture and Use. This appendix enumerates new {{siteref("Structural_Element", "structural elements")|safe}} in the Scaling model, relative to the Static Normals model, which provide the Scaling model with emissions estimates in those two sectors.

The new structural elements are:

  • Cattle Static Normals
  • Bovaer Adoption Limit
  • Bovaer Production Emissions
  • Bovaer Farm Subsidy
  • Bovaer Purchase Cost
  • Bovaer Monitoring

[edit: The interaction of these elements can be visualized with the new Model Explorer.]

Cattle Population Static Normal

This structural element makes static normal estimates of cattle populations and Enteric Fermentation {{siteref("Emission_Factor", "emission factors")|safe}} to define several unknown variables, including:

  1. head counts
  2. NIR2025 Enteric Fermentation emission factors
  3. NIR2025 overall Enteric Fermentation emissions (including all livestock)
  4. Methane abated through the administration of Bovaer
Estimates are made for each of 8 cattle populations (beef cows, dairy cows, beef heifers for replacement, beef heifers for slaughter, dairy heifers, calves, steers, and bulls), and each province and territory.

The Static Normals model of Enteric Fermentation estimated methane emission directly. In the Scaling model, this element estimates Enteric Fermentation emissions as the sum-product of the number of cattle of each type, and the emission factor of each type. Details of the probabilistic model are provided below in Appendix 2: Probabilistic Modelling of Cattle Populations and Enteric Fermentation Emission Factors. In the future, when PlanZero modelling progresses beyond static normals, the inclusion of this granular data will allow PlanZero to use more data and make more accurate short-term predictions.

Bovaer Adoption Limit

Previously when modelling Bovaer deterministically, this element provided an assumption that no more than 5.0 percent / year of farmers would switch to administering Bovaer in any given year, but that adoption could ultimately rise to 99.0%, (the 1% remainder being organic farmers who can't adopt it).

Here, when used in a probabilistic model, this element provides instead an assumption that 2.5-10.0% of farmers will switch to Bovaer in any given year, and it will vary from year to year within that range. Currently no statistically significant number of farms administer Bovaer, so the projected adoption starts at 0%. The same maximum of 99% remains, to account for organic farms.

Bovaer Production Emissions

In deterministic models, this element represented an assumption that the production and distribution of Bovaer incurred an extra 45.0 kg {{CO2}} / head / year in the Other Product Manufacture and Use sector.

In the probabilistic model, the amount is based instead on a "20-50x" reduction in CO2e compared with methane emitted from cattle without Bovaer. This was the literal suggestion from an LLM-AI chat earlier this year, which was the original source for the 45.0 kg {{CO2}} / head / year value. For each sampled scenario, a reduction factor is sampled uniformly from 20 to 50. This element does not reflect manufacturer or 3rd party estimates of emission rates nor does it include any change in production emission efficiency over time.

This element also estimates the emission amounts themselves, not just the emission factor. These emissions are added to the emissions of the Other Product Manufacture and Use sector, which is otherwise estimated as it was in the Static Normals model.

Bovaer Farm Subsidy

The Bovaer Farm Subsidy element defines the dynamics of farms switching to Bovaer administration, based on (a) the availability of funds in a subsidy program, (b) an assumption of 160 cattle per farm, and (c) a taxable subsidy of $5000 per-farm, per-year. It makes no difference to the model, but in reality I'd imagine the subsidy might well be tied to head counts rather than being a flat rate per farm.

Bovaer Purchase Cost

The Bovaer Purchase Cost element represents an assumption that Bovaer purchase costs are borne directly by a hypothetical government program, at rates of $.20/head/day for calves up to $.50/head/day for bulls and mature cows. The rates were estimated based on this site, and should be modelled with some uncertainty but currently are not.

Bovaer Monitoring

As in the deterministic case, the Bovaer Monitoring element represents an assumption that administration and monitoring costs amount to 1,000.0 CAD / farm / year for paperwork and 3,000.0 CAD / farm / year for on-site inspection. When used in probabilistic modelling, this element furthermore estimates that these amounts are paid in labour costs, from which income tax is collected back at a rate of 25%.

Appendix 2: Probabilistic Modelling of Cattle Populations and Enteric Fermentation Emission Factors

The Static Normals model (specifically with Dec 2024 data cutoff) modelled every sector's national and regional (provincial and territorial) emissions in the same way, described in Appendix 2 of the post introducing that model. The Scaling model handles Enteric Emissions differently, in order to introduce variables that could be acted-upon to simulate the effects of scaling Bovaer adoption.

Also, in order to make reasonable inferences of the cattle-type populations and of their respective emission factors, this post draws on more data sources. Statistics Canada publishes national and regional cattle populations for each January 1 and July 1 since the 1930s. That data was described and first used in PlanZero in this post, which explored the causes of Enteric Fermentation emissions. The NIR-2025 document also publishes the emission factors it has used for cattle and other livestock for each year covered, from 1990-2023. Cattle population counts were adapted for inference by averaging January and July headcounts to get annual headcounts.

As noted in the previous post on Enteric Fermentation emissions, cattle are responsible for 96-97% of the national total in that sector, with the rest coming from other livestock types. This model assumes they contribute 100%. Future modelling work could compensate for this 3-4% gap in a few ways, but it isn't done yet.

Formally, the Enteric Fermentation emissions, and cattle populations, and emissions factors in the Scaling model are estimated as the posterior distributions in this model after observing data up until Dec 31, 2024 from the NIR and StatsCan data sources.

Unknown Variable and Distribution Description
{{latex("\\phi_{(c,r)} ~\\sim~ \\mathrm{Lognormal}(-1, 2)")}} Scaled cattle headcount means, by cattle type {{latex("c")}} and region {{latex("r")}}. The scaling factor was the inverse of the largest recorded cattle-type headcount in the historical data, so that the scaled, observed cattle headcounts would lie between 0 and 1.
{{latex("\\beta_{(c,r)} ~\\sim~ \\mathrm{Lognormal}(-1, 1)")}} Scaled cattle headcount standard deviations, by cattle type {{latex("c")}} and region {{latex("r")}}.
{{latex("h_{(c,r)} ~\\sim~ \\mathcal{N}(\\phi_{(c, r)}, \\beta_{(c, r)})")}} Observable scaled cattle headcount, by cattle type {{latex("c")}} and region {{latex("r")}}.
{{latex("\\gamma_{(c)} ~\\sim~ \\mathcal{N}(A, B^2)")}} A latent emission factor {{latex("c")}} was defined for each cattle type, and used across regions. It was modelled as coming a-priori from a normal distribution around {{latex("A")}}, the approximate NIR-published means over time, and {{latex("B^2")}}, the approximate variance over time.
{{latex("f_{(c)} ~\\sim~ \\mathcal{N}(\\gamma_{(c)}, 20)")}} Observable emission factor, defined for each cattle type.
{{latex("\\sigma_{(r)} ~\\sim~ \\mathrm{Lognormal}(-1, 0.7)")}} A-priori, the variance in scaled regional emissions over years is not expected to be super-tiny, nor much larger than 1.
{{latex("\\sigma_{()} ~\\sim~ \\mathrm{Lognormal}(-1, 0.7)")}} A-priori, the variance in scaled national emissions over years is not expected to be super-tiny, nor much larger than 1.
{{latex("X_{(r,t)} ~\\sim~ \\mathcal{N}( \\sum_c h_{(c,r)} f_{(c)}, \\sigma_{(r)}^2)")}} Observable scaled NIR emissions for the region ({{latex("r")}}), and annual time period ({{latex("t")}}). Note that the distribution is "static", in that it is the same for all annual time periods.
{{latex("Y_{(t)} ~\\sim~ \\mathcal{N}( \\sum_{r \\in \\mathrm{regions}} \\sum_c h_{(c,r)} f_{(c)}, \\sigma_{(s,g)}^2)")}} Observable scaled national-total emissions for Enteric Fermentation emissions (methane). Note that the distribution is static, in that it is the same for all annual time periods.

Posterior estimates of the cattle population and emission factors were obtained using NumPyro's NUTS sampler and drawing 500 samples after warming up for 250.
