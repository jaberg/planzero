New Model Metric: Net-Zero-2050

Canada's federal government set a target to achieve net zero emissions by 2050. PlanZero's predictive models can estimate the probability of Canada's emissions being less than or equal to zero in year 2050. This post announces the addition of a new "NetZero-2050" evaluation metric on what's now the "Metrics" tab. (It used to be called "Predictions", but metrics would have been a better name for it even before adding this new metric, which has nothing to do with prediction evaluation.)

The NetZero-2050 evaluation metric that now appears on the metrics page features the following ranking of predictive probabilistic models.
Name Short Description probability of annual net emissions <= 0 (higher is better, 1 is perfect)}
Scaling Hypothetical scaling of emission-reduction measures within an otherwise non-time-varying economy 6.09e-48
Static Normals (data-cutoff: 2024-12-31) Very simple baseline model - non-time-varying estimates 2.38e-49

These numbers were estimated by averaging over the posterior samples generated by inference in these models, and estimating the probability of non-positive net emissions in year 2050 for each sampled scenario. Net emissions are calculated by summing over all sectors, excluding the so-called LULUCF sectors related to land-use, land-use change, and forestry.

The static normals model (Static Normals with data-cutoff 2024-12-31) reflects an assumption that emissions have always been kind of the same, and continue unchanged. It assesses net-zero as improbable in the extreme (probability {{latex("2.38 x 10^{-49}")}}, which is about as likely as being dealt six consecutive royal flushes off the top of a fair perfectly shuffled decks of cards). If anything, it is validating of the computations in PlanZero that this probability is estimated to be minuscule, because if emissions stay at or near what they have been for the last few decades, that's not net zero. Also validating of the computations in PlanZero is the result that the scaling model makes net zero by 2050 more likely, relative to the static normals model. The scaling model also assesses net-zero by 2050 as nearly impossible (probability {{latex("6.09x10^{-48}")}}) but for what it's worth, that's 25x more likely than under the static normals model.

Over many PlanZero posts to come, I hope the scaling model can be extended with a set of strategies that reflects what's possible by scaling existing relatively low-cost, low-risk measures, such as renewable electricity generation, electric vehicles, heat pumps, and operational improvements in oil and gas extraction. I would like for PlanZero's "Scaling" model to evolve into a credible estimator of how far such measures will go.

Until then,

- James Bergstra
